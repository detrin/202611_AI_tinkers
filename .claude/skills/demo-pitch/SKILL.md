# Demo pitch — AI Tinkerers Prague, "AI Agents for the Rest of Us"

## What the judges score

Quoted from the event page. Every slide and every sentence must serve one of these.

1. "Does it solve a real problem for a real person? What's your personal insight into the problem?"
2. "Does it work? What did you learn during the hack from your peers? How well did you use the tools available?"
3. "Would people outside tech actually use it?"

Format: "a few minutes" per team, live only, "no pre-recorded videos". Sponsors on the jury: Apify, Featherless, PostHog. The challenge wording: "Think of someone you know and a task they hate doing. Build an agent that does it for them."

## Structure (aim for 3 minutes, ask organizers for the exact limit)

| Time | Beat | Content |
|------|------|---------|
| 0:00 | Person | One named real person and their morning. Not "users". One sentence on why choosing clothes costs them something. |
| 0:25 | Insight | What we noticed that others miss: the answer is already in the calendar, the weather and the route. Nobody should have to assemble it at 7am. |
| 0:45 | Live demo | Phone on screen. Today's real calendar, today's real Prague weather, the outfit it picked and the reason in one line. Then change one input live (add a meeting, or a bike commute) and show the outfit change. |
| 2:10 | How | One diagram: sources → Apify actors → agent → outfit. Name each sponsor tool once, with what it does for us. |
| 2:35 | Learned | One specific thing a teammate or workshop taught us today. |
| 2:50 | Close | Who else would use it tomorrow, in one sentence. Stop. |

The demo is the pitch. Slides exist only for the person, the diagram and the close: three slides at most.

## Demo safety

- The live change of input is the proof it is not canned. Rehearse it at least three times.
- Keep the last good response cached in the app so a dead network still shows a real result; say out loud if you fall back to it.
- One phone mirrored to the projector, do-not-disturb on, brightness up, screen lock off.
- One speaker, one driver. The driver never talks.

## Writing rules

- Concrete nouns and numbers. "Three meetings, 6°C, 20 minutes on a bike" beats "context-aware recommendations".
- Banned: revolutionize, seamless, leverage, empower, unlock, game-changer, AI-powered, smart, effortless, "imagine a world", rhetorical questions as openers, rule-of-three adjective lists, emoji on slides.
- One idea per slide, at most 12 words, no bullet lists. If a slide needs bullets it is two slides or zero.
- Say what it does not do yet when asked. Judges trust teams that know their limits.
- Read every line aloud. Cut anything nobody would say to a friend.

## Slide look

Use the tokens in the `product-ui` skill so the deck, the PWA and the website look like one product. Large type, one focal element, generous empty space, real screenshots of the running app rather than mockups.
